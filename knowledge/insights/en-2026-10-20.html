Root Causes of Color Complaints and Fixes: Seal the Sample Before Talking Color

Bulk differing from sample usually comes from no sealed sample or machine drift. The fix: sealed sample as the only standard, ICC curves to lock drift, standardized fixing.

首页 > 知识库 > 每日知识快报 > Root Causes of Color Complaints and Fixes: Seal the Sample Before Talking Color

Bulk differing from the approved sample is the top complaint in printing OEM, but the root cause almost always falls into two types: no sealed sample, or uncontrolled machine drift. Many disputes are avoidable — pin the standard at the very start.

Root-cause breakdown

  • No seal: no single reference, bulk judged by eye, subjective and drifting, he-said-she-said in disputes.
  • Machine drift: different machines/batches with inconsistent curves output different colors, obvious on cross-line reorders.
  • Random fixing: varying steaming/washing shifts color later; right at shipment, off in the customer's hand.

Three-step fix

1. Seal the sample: first piece or supplied sample sealed as the only bulk standard, reference when disputed.

2. Curve lock: independent ICC curve per line for cross-machine consistency, reorders not by memory.

3. Standardized fixing: fixed steaming/washing/finishing, no color shift, shipment equals receipt.

Done well, batch ΔE stays under 1.5 — sample equals bulk.

Data and capacity

Stability relies on system, not individuals: 8 inks at 16.7M colors base, ICC curves quantify and lock device differences; six widths 200-320CM full-width direct printing avoids micro-seam color differences; grade 4+ fastness resists sun and washing.

Practical notes

Write ΔE<1.5 acceptance and reorder-consistency clauses; archive the sealed sample through project end; first-piece check against the seal, stop the line on mismatch. Move from people-control to system-control.

Fit and limits

Seal + ICC are critical for high-fidelity curtains, wallcoverings, home textiles and projects; plain colors may simplify but sealing is still advised.

Selection Checklist

Check each item before ordering to reduce rework and color drift:

  • Seal sample immediately after approval as the only bulk standard
  • Require per-line independent ICC curves to lock drift
  • Fix steaming/washing/finishing params to stop later shift
  • Contract ΔE<1.5 acceptance and reorder-consistency
  • Archive the seal through project/order end
  • First-piece check vs seal; stop line on mismatch
  • Confirm grade 4+ fastness, sun/wash resistant
  • Shift color control from people to system

Common Myths

Several misconceptions circulate in the industry; clarifying them helps rational selection:

误区：A master's eye is enough

正解：Subjective and drifting; seal + ICC pin the standard.

误区：Same factory, same device, no drift

正解：Batch curves can still drift; ICC must quantify and lock.

误区：Right at shipment means fine

正解：Unfixed params shift later; off at customer, untraceable.

常见问题

Most common root cause of color complaints?

No sealed sample (no single standard) or uncontrolled machine drift.

How to control color difference?

Sealed sample as the only standard, per-line ICC curves to lock drift, standardized fixing for batch ΔE<1.5.

Why is random fixing dangerous?

Varying steaming/washing shifts color later; right at ship, off at receipt, hard to trace.

How to keep reorders same color?

Archived seal + per-line ICC curves, consistent across machines, not by memory.

What to write in the contract?

ΔE<1.5 acceptance, reorder-consistency clause, seal archive period.

Do plain colors need sealing?

Low-fidelity can simplify but sealing is still advised as backup.
