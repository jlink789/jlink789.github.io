How Fastness Is Actually Achieved

Fastness is a system property, and pre-treatment sets the ceiling. We choose the process by fabric: disperse / heat-transfer sublimation for polyester, where color fixes into the fiber and stays stable through washing and light exposure; polyester printing for blends, with pre-treatment and fixation tuned to the greige. The same artwork yields different fastness on different base cloth, so we confirm fabric, gsm and end use — sun exposure or repeated laundering — at order review, validate by sampling, and only then release bulk against the sealed sample.

What the 7 Per-Meter Checks Cover

Color difference, pattern deviation, gsm, width, color fastness, shrinkage and surface defects, checked on every meter by machine plus human visual inspection, double-reviewed rather than spot-checked. For one-piece full-width curtain, wallcovering and bedding orders with no cuts, per-meter checking stops a defect from accumulating across the roll — a deviation is caught on the spot.

Color drift and batch control: three questions we are asked most

QBatch one and batch two never match — how do you control it?

ABy system, not by feel. The approved sample is sealed with its colour card and process parameters on file; reorders pull the same record and match to the sealed standard at ΔE 1.5.

QDo different substrates or grammages drift?

AThey can, so we file each substrate separately — ink load and sizing recorded for chenille, coated blackout and non-woven. A new substrate gets a trial run first.

QWhat happens if a customer reports a shade complaint?

AWe start from the sealed sample: if our process drifted we reprint, if the greige batch differs we adjust and re-approve a small lot. The rule is written into the order.
