How to Judge Whether a Digital Printing Mill Is Reliable?

We at J.LINK TEXTILE suggest four verifiable tests: are the lines in-house, are the widths routinely scheduled, are QC figures actually quoted, and are small runs accepted. We meet all four.

首页 > 知识库 > How to Judge Whether a Digital Printing Mill Is Reliable?

We at J.LINK TEXTILE have handled many orders that came back after a cheaper quote went wrong. The fastest way to judge a mill is to translate 'reliable' into four questions you can verify on the spot, rather than admiring a sample book. We answer all four below, and buyers are welcome to use the same list on any other supplier.

Four Checks You Can Verify On the Spot

  • In-house or outsourced：Ask to see running orders and ask where printing, transfer and finishing each happen; all three are in our Keqiao plant.
  • Are widths routine：Ask how many widths are scheduled consistently; we run six standards 200/220/240/260/280/320CM, with 320CM in one full-width pass.
  • Are QC figures quoted：Ask batch ΔE, approved-sample ΔE, width tolerance, shrinkage and fastness: we state ΔE ≤ 2.0 / ≤ 1.5 / ±2CM / ≤3% / wash ≥ grade 4, light ≥ grade 5.
  • Full or spot inspection：We run 7 checks on every meter with double review, and ship the records with the goods.
  • Are small runs accepted：We start at 1 meter with free sampling of 1-2 designs, so a sample comes before any bulk discussion.

One More Test: Do Sampling and Bulk Share the Same Criteria

Many mills proof in ideal conditions and then compress cost on bulk, which is exactly where color drift appears. We seal a sample at proofing, compare finished goods at ΔE ≤ 1.5 to that sample under the same batch and light source, keep 720dpi output unchanged, and supply OEKO-TEX certification and inspection records — so sample and bulk can be measured with the same ruler.

Three Questions to Ask Us on a Line Visit

One: which workshop runs this order and can I see it in production. Two: which width do you recommend for my finished size, and why. Three: if finished goods exceed ΔE ≤ 1.5 against the approved sample, how is it handled. A mill that answers cleanly is usually easy to work with afterwards.
