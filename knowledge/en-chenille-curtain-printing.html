Chenille Curtain Printing: Difficulties & Process Adaptation for Plush Fabric

Chenille is thick and uneven in ink uptake, a printing challenge. J.LINK uses wide-format digital printing + pretreatment adaptation, seamless, stable color, 1-meter minimum.

首页 > 知识库 > Chenille Curtain Printing: Difficulties & Process Adaptation for Plush Fabric

Chenille is loved in mid-high-end curtains for its thick, premium pile, but that very pile is the printing challenge: the nap makes ink uneven and the motif blurry. Chenille curtain printing is won by pretreatment and process adaptation.

Chenille Printing Difficulties

  • Uneven ink uptake: uneven nap makes ink land inconsistently, easy streaks
  • Poor penetration: thick pile keeps ink from base, rubs off
  • Pile direction: with/against nap shows different color for same design
  • Drape keeping: printing must not stiffen the pile and lose hang

Hard printing alone cannot solve these; adapt from both pretreatment and process.

Adaptation Thinking

Table 1: Chenille curtain printing adaptation

DifficultyAdaptationEffect
Uneven uptakeSize to level pileEven ink
Poor penetrationPretreat to penetrate + temp fixGrade 4+ fastness
Pile directionFixed feed directionConsistent color
DrapeLight set to keep pileNatural hang

Process Selection

  • Polyester chenille: heat transfer / disperse, color + sun
  • Blend chenille: by composition choose wide-format digital printing or disperse
  • Large chenille curtain: wide-format seamless, continuous pile no seam

Note: confirm composition and pile direction before printing to avoid color difference and fading.

Pretreatment Notes

  • Sizing: level the pile, reduce streaks
  • Penetration aid: agent deepens ink to base
  • Fixing: standardized steam/wash/set, stable fastness
  • Sampling: free sampling before bulk, confirm pile color then scale
